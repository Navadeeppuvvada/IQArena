What is Intelligence Quotient?

The science behind measuring human intelligence

IQ (Intelligence Quotient) is a measure of human cognitive ability and potential. It assesses key faculties such as fluid reasoning, spatial processing, working memory, and quantitative reasoning.

The concept was pioneered by William Stern in 1912 and refined by Alfred Binet. Today, it is the most statistically valid metric for predicting academic success and job performance.

Modern IQ tests follow a Normal Distribution (Bell Curve). The average is fixed at 100, with a standard deviation of 15, meaning 68% of people fall within the 85-115 range.

Scientific Calculation Methodology

Based on the Deviation IQ Standard (Wechsler/Stanford-Binet)

IQ = 100 + (15 × z-score)

Where z-score = (Raw Score - Population Mean) / Standard Deviation

Deviation IQ Model

Unlike older "Mental Age" formulas, our test uses the modern Deviation IQ method. This compares your performance against a standardized sample of peers in your age group, ensuring accuracy across all life stages.

Key Cognitive Domains

Our algorithm weighs four primary indices: Fluid Reasoning, Working Memory, Processing Speed, and Visual-Spatial Processing.

5 Common Myths About IQ

Separating fact from fiction

🧬 Myth 1: You're born with a fixed IQ

Fact: Your brain can grow and change! Like a muscle, you can strengthen your mind through learning, new experiences, and practice.

💼 Myth 2: High IQ = Success

Fact: Being smart isn't enough. Hard work, social skills, and not giving up are often more important for doing well in life.

📚 Myth 3: IQ tests check what you know

Fact: IQ tests aren't like school exams. They don't ask facts. They just check how well you can solve puzzles.

🧠 Myth 4: We only use 10% of our brain

Fact: That's a myth! We use our whole brain all the time, even when we are sleeping.

🎓 Myth 5: Low IQ means you can't learn

Fact: IQ just measures how fast you pick things up. Everyone can learn difficult things—some people just might need a little more time or a different way of being taught.
